# AI Risk Management

## Overview

Artificial Intelligence introduces risks that may differ from traditional technology risk.

AI risk management should consider the complete lifecycle of an AI capability, from initial concept and data selection through implementation, operation and eventual retirement.

The objective is to identify and manage risks while enabling appropriate business value.

---

## AI Risk Categories

AI use cases should be assessed across multiple dimensions.

### Security Risk

Consider:

- Unauthorised access
- Prompt injection
- Data leakage
- Model manipulation
- Malicious use
- Compromised AI services
- Insecure integrations

### Privacy Risk

Consider:

- Personal information
- Sensitive information
- Data retention
- Data residency
- Secondary use of data
- Privacy obligations

### Accuracy and Reliability

AI outputs may be incorrect, incomplete or misleading.

Consider:

- Hallucinations
- Data quality
- Model limitations
- Output validation
- Human verification
- Appropriate use cases

### Bias and Fairness

AI systems may produce outcomes that are biased or unfair.

Consider:

- Training data
- Model behaviour
- User populations
- Decision impact
- Testing and monitoring

### Intellectual Property

Consider:

- Copyright
- Confidential information
- Proprietary information
- Training data
- AI-generated content
- Ownership of outputs

### Regulatory and Legal Risk

AI use should consider applicable:

- Legislation
- Regulatory requirements
- Industry obligations
- Contractual requirements
- Internal policies

### Third-Party Risk

AI solutions may rely on external providers, models, APIs or infrastructure.

Consider:

- Supplier security
- Data handling
- Service availability
- Model changes
- Subprocessors
- Contractual protections
- Exit arrangements

---

## Risk Assessment Model

A simple assessment can consider:

**Likelihood × Impact = Inherent Risk**

Controls are then applied to determine:

**Residual Risk**

The assessment should consider both the probability of an adverse outcome and the potential business impact.

---

## AI Risk Tiers

| Tier | Example | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | Internal productivity assistance | Standard controls |
| **Medium** | Internal decision support | Risk assessment and monitoring |
| **High** | Customer-impacting AI | Formal approval and human oversight |
| **Critical** | Material automated decisions | Enhanced governance and executive oversight |

---

## AI Use Case Assessment

Before implementation, an AI use case should answer:

1. What problem are we solving?
2. What value will AI provide?
3. What information will be used?
4. Who will use the capability?
5. Who could be affected by the outcome?
6. What could go wrong?
7. What controls are required?
8. Who owns the risk?
9. How will the solution be monitored?
10. What happens if the AI capability needs to be withdrawn?

---

## Human Oversight

Human oversight should be proportionate to the potential impact of an AI-enabled decision.

Higher-risk use cases may require:

- Human approval
- Review of AI outputs
- Ability to override decisions
- Escalation procedures
- Audit trails
- Periodic review

AI should not automatically be treated as the final decision-maker simply because it can produce an answer.

## AI Risk Register

Organisations should maintain visibility of significant AI use cases and their associated risks.

A practical register could include:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| AI Use Case | Customer service assistant |
| Business Owner | Customer Operations |
| Technology | Generative AI platform |
| Data Classification | Internal |
| Risk Tier | Medium |
| Key Risks | Accuracy / Privacy |
| Controls | Access control / Human review |
| Residual Risk | Medium |
| Approval | Executive / Risk |
| Review Date | Annual |

---

## Continuous Monitoring

AI risk does not end when a system is approved.

Ongoing monitoring should consider:

- Model performance
- Unexpected outputs
- Security incidents
- Data changes
- Changes to the underlying model
- User behaviour
- Regulatory developments
- Emerging threats

---

## Executive Perspective

AI risk management should enable informed decision-making.

The objective is not to ask:

> "Is AI safe?"

Instead, organisations should ask:

> **"What are the risks associated with this particular AI use case, and are we comfortable with the residual risk?"**

This allows organisations to adopt AI according to their risk appetite while maintaining appropriate governance.
