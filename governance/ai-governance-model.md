# AI Governance Model

## Overview

Artificial Intelligence creates significant opportunities for organisations to improve productivity, decision-making, customer experience and operational efficiency.

However, AI adoption introduces new risks relating to data, privacy, security, accuracy, intellectual property, regulatory obligations and organisational accountability.

An effective AI governance model should enable responsible adoption while avoiding unnecessary barriers to innovation.

---

## Governance Principles

The AI governance model is based on the following principles:

- **Business value** — AI initiatives should have a clearly defined business purpose.
- **Risk-based governance** — Controls should be proportionate to the potential impact of the AI use case.
- **Human accountability** — Appropriate human oversight should remain in place for material decisions.
- **Security by design** — AI solutions should incorporate security throughout their lifecycle.
- **Privacy by design** — Personal and sensitive information should be appropriately protected.
- **Transparency** — Users should understand when and how AI is being used where appropriate.
- **Data governance** — AI outputs are only as reliable as the data and processes supporting them.
- **Continuous monitoring** — AI solutions should be monitored after implementation, not simply approved once.
- **Responsible innovation** — Governance should enable safe experimentation rather than unnecessarily preventing adoption.

## AI Governance Lifecycle

A practical AI governance lifecycle is:

**Identify → Assess → Approve → Implement → Monitor → Review**

### Identify

Understand:

- What AI capability is being proposed
- The business problem being addressed
- Users and stakeholders
- Data involved
- Technology involved
- Expected benefits

### Assess

Consider:

- Security risk
- Privacy risk
- Data sensitivity
- Regulatory requirements
- Accuracy and reliability
- Bias and fairness
- Intellectual property
- Third-party risk
- Business impact

### Approve

Determine the appropriate level of governance based on the risk and intended use.

Potential outcomes include:

- Approved
- Approved with conditions
- Requires additional assessment
- Restricted
- Not approved

### Implement

Ensure appropriate controls are implemented before production use.

These may include:

- Access controls
- Data protection
- Human oversight
- Logging
- Monitoring
- User training
- Acceptable use requirements

### Monitor

Monitor:

- Usage
- Performance
- Security
- Data handling
- Unexpected behaviour
- Incidents
- User feedback

### Review

AI use cases should be periodically reviewed as:

- Technology changes
- Regulations evolve
- Business use changes
- New risks emerge
- Models are updated

## Governance Tiers

Not every AI use case requires the same level of governance.

| Tier | Example | Governance |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | Productivity assistance | Standard acceptable-use controls |
| **Medium** | Internal decision support | Risk assessment + monitoring |
| **High** | Customer or employee-impacting decisions | Formal assessment + human oversight |
| **Critical** | Material automated decisions | Executive governance + enhanced assurance |

---

## Roles and Responsibilities

AI governance should involve multiple functions.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive Leadership | Risk appetite and strategic direction |
| Technology | Architecture, implementation and technical controls |
| Information Security | Security risk and control assessment |
| Privacy / Legal | Privacy and regulatory considerations |
| Risk | Enterprise risk alignment |
| Business Owner | Business purpose and accountability |
| Users | Responsible use and adherence to policy |

---

## Executive Perspective

AI governance should not be viewed as a mechanism for stopping AI adoption.

The objective is to establish a framework that allows organisations to answer:

> **What AI are we using, why are we using it, what could go wrong, and what controls are required?**

Good governance should create the confidence required to adopt AI at scale.
