# AI Governance Metrics

## Overview

Effective AI governance requires visibility of both the value created by AI and the risks introduced by its use.

Metrics should help executives understand:

- How quickly AI is being adopted
- Where AI is creating value
- Whether risks are being managed
- Whether governance controls are operating effectively

---

## Four Metric Categories

A practical AI governance dashboard can be structured around:

**Adoption + Value + Risk + Governance**

---

## 1. AI Adoption

Measures how AI is being adopted across the organisation.

Examples:

- Number of active AI users
- Percentage of employees using approved AI tools
- Number of approved AI use cases
- AI usage frequency
- Training completion
- Number of business units using AI

### Example KPI

**AI Adoption Rate**

Percentage of eligible employees actively using approved AI capabilities.

---

## 2. Business Value

Measures whether AI is delivering meaningful outcomes.

Examples:

- Hours saved
- Process cycle-time reduction
- Cost reduction
- Revenue contribution
- Productivity improvement
- Customer experience improvement
- Manual processes automated

### Example KPI

**Productivity Hours Saved**

Estimated employee hours saved through approved AI-enabled processes.

---

## 3. AI Risk

Measures the organisation's exposure to AI-related risk.

Examples:

- Number of high-risk AI use cases
- Number of critical AI risks
- AI-related incidents
- Data leakage events
- Policy violations
- Security events
- High-risk use cases without completed assessments

### Example KPI

**High-Risk AI Use Cases**

Number of AI use cases classified as high or critical risk.

---

## 4. Governance Effectiveness

Measures whether governance processes are operating as intended.

Examples:

- Percentage of AI use cases risk assessed
- Percentage of high-risk use cases formally approved
- Policy compliance
- AI review completion
- Control testing results
- Outstanding remediation actions
- Supplier assessments completed

### Example KPI

**AI Governance Coverage**

Percentage of known AI use cases that have completed the required governance assessment.

---

## Executive Dashboard

A practical executive dashboard could include:

| Category | Metric | Trend | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adoption | Active AI Users | ↑ | Increasing |
| Adoption | Approved Use Cases | ↑ | Increasing |
| Value | Hours Saved | ↑ | Increasing |
| Value | Productivity Improvement | ↑ | Increasing |
| Risk | High-Risk Use Cases | → | Controlled |
| Risk | AI Incidents | ↓ | Minimal |
| Governance | Risk Assessment Coverage | ↑ | 100% |
| Governance | Policy Compliance | ↑ | >95% |
| Governance | Open Remediation Actions | ↓ | Reducing |

---

## Avoiding Vanity Metrics

AI reporting should avoid focusing solely on activity.

For example:

> "Employees generated 500,000 AI prompts."

This demonstrates usage but does not demonstrate business value.

A more useful measure would be:

> "AI-enabled processes reduced average processing time by 22% while maintaining required control effectiveness."

The objective is to demonstrate:

**Adoption → Value → Risk → Outcome**

---

## Risk Appetite Metrics

Executives should have visibility of AI activity relative to organisational risk appetite.

Examples include:

- Number of critical AI use cases
- Number of policy exceptions
- Number of unresolved AI risks
- Number of AI incidents
- Number of unapproved AI services
- Percentage of high-risk AI systems with human oversight

---

## AI Portfolio Reporting

As AI adoption increases, organisations should maintain visibility of the overall AI portfolio.

A portfolio view could include:

| Use Case | Owner | Risk | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Internal Assistant | IT | Low | High | Production |
| Customer Support AI | Operations | Medium | High | Pilot |
| Document Processing | Finance | Medium | Medium | Production |
| Automated Decisioning | Lending | High | High | Assessment |

---

## Board Reporting

Board-level reporting should focus on:

### Strategic

- Where is AI creating value?
- How is AI changing the organisation?
- What investment is required?

### Risk

- What are the material AI risks?
- Are controls effective?
- Are there regulatory concerns?

### Governance

- Are high-risk use cases appropriately governed?
- Are policies being followed?
- Are significant incidents occurring?

### Resilience

- Are critical AI dependencies understood?
- What happens if an AI provider becomes unavailable?
- Are alternative processes available?

---

## Executive Perspective

The purpose of AI metrics is not to create more reporting.

The purpose is to provide enough information for leadership to make informed decisions.

A mature AI dashboard should allow executives to answer:

> **Are we adopting AI?**

> **Are we getting value from it?**

> **Are we managing the risks?**

> **Is our governance working?**

The ultimate objective is:

**More value + controlled risk + measurable outcomes**
